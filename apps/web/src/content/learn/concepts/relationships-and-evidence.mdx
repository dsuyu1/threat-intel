# Relationships & evidence

Relationships are the “edges” of the [knowledge graph](/learn/concepts/knowledge-graph): they connect two [entities](/learn/concepts/entities).
Evidence is what makes those edges verifiable.

## Relationship shape

A relationship is defined by:

- `type`: the meaning of the connection (e.g. `actor_uses_technique`)
- `fromKind/fromId` → `toKind/toId`: the directed endpoints
- `firstSeen` / `lastSeen`: time bounds for when the relationship was observed
- `support`: how much backing the relationship has (higher = stronger signal)
- `confidence`: 0–1 quality/probability signal (higher = more likely correct)
- `attributes`: provenance metadata (where it came from, ids from upstream systems, etc.)

Example (conceptually):

- `type="actor_uses_technique"`
- `fromKind="actor"`, `fromId="G0102"`
- `toKind="technique"`, `toId="T1059"`

## Support vs confidence

These two numbers answer different questions:

- **Confidence (0–1)**: “How likely is this edge correct?”
- **Support (≥ 0)**: “How much backing does this edge have?”

In practice:

- A structured source (like STIX) may yield **high confidence** edges with consistent **support**.
- An extracted claim from a single article might be **high confidence** but low support (or vice versa).

Some ranking logic multiplies them (e.g. `support * confidence`) to prioritize “strong and confident” edges.

## Evidence

Evidence is attached to a relationship as:

- A source **URL**
- A short **excerpt**
- Optionally, a **publish timestamp**
- A per-evidence **confidence**

Evidence answers: “Why does the system think this relationship exists?”

<Callout type="tip">
If an edge surprises you, look for its evidence first. Evidence is the fastest way to confirm, refute, or adjust trust.
</Callout>

## Provenance (attributes)

Not every relationship has a readable excerpt — especially edges imported from structured sources.
In those cases, the system still stores provenance in `attributes` (for example, the upstream relationship id and source kind),
so you can trace where it came from.
